- [#242] [REQ-INSP-001] A JPEG file of more than 100 scans, or a TIFF file whose tile or strip holds more pixels than its image needs (more than the larger of 1024 × 1024 and the image with each side rounded up to a multiple of 16) or that gives the size of its tiles or strips twice, is now refused with AOI-INSP-006 before it is decoded. Before, such a file was decoded and judged, and held the run for as long as the decode took (about 20 s for a 0.16 MB JPEG repeating one scan 2,000 times, and longer with more) or took about 1 GB of memory (a 64 × 64 TIFF declaring one 16000 × 16000 tile; a strip far taller than its image had OpenCV allocate up to 1 GiB, of which only the image's rows were filled). Scans are counted as the decoder reads them, so bytes after the image, such as the video a phone keeps in a motion photo, are not counted. Progressive JPEGs as cameras and image tools save them (10 scans) and TIFFs whose tiles or strips fit their image still open. A strip TIFF whose RowsPerStrip is a constant well above a short image's height, such as 2,048 rows per strip for a 640 × 480 image, is refused as well, although it decodes cheaply; no TIFF that Pillow, tifffile or OpenCV wrote in testing was refused.
